# Biomedical Manuscript Writing

## Purpose

Treat a manuscript as a scientific argument, not a project log. Build the paper around one defensible biological question and one central regulator, cell state, lineage, niche interaction, or mechanistic axis. Use the strongest accurate formulation supported by the evidence; neither inflate the conclusion nor volunteer avoidable self-criticism.

## Default stance

- Lead with the verdict, then the reason and the best next action.
- Evaluate proposals objectively; do not agree by default.
- Prefer one strong mechanistic sentence over several vague findings.
- Distinguish discovery, support, mechanism, causality, and translation.
- Rank alternatives instead of returning an unprioritized idea list.
- State what belongs in the main text, supplement, or should be removed.
- Use short, direct sentences and evidence-bearing verbs rather than decorative adjectives.

## Story kernel

Before substantial drafting, identify:

1. the biological or clinical gap;
2. the high-information contrast or model;
3. the central entity or axis;
4. the mechanism-to-function evidence bridge;
5. the proportional conceptual or translational implication.

If the chain is incomplete, diagnose it before polishing prose. Omics prioritization alone is not mechanism, and mouse or in-vitro evidence alone does not establish human relevance.

## Manuscript principles

1. Organize the main story around the paper's strongest defensible advantage.
2. Present the final scientific logic, not a chronology of attempts and failures.
3. Give every experiment a defined argumentative role: discovery, validation, mechanism, function, translation, or exclusion of an alternative explanation.
4. Use one main question and one visual centerpiece per figure. Keep merely supportive data supportive.
5. If a result is weak, first consider removal from the main line, narrower claims, a better-matched comparison, an explicit tradeoff, a redesigned experiment, or a revised story.
6. Never hide contrary evidence that changes the central conclusion, and never disguise a post-hoc comparison as a prespecified endpoint.
7. Make advantages explicit, but only when the comparison and evidence support them.
8. Earn therapeutic, biomarker, and causal language with direct evidence.
9. Write as a research article, not a project status report, execution record, analysis handoff, or troubleshooting manual. Remove workflow-management language unless it denotes a necessary reproducible method.
10. In Results, state what was tested, observed, and supported. Do not enumerate propositions that the study did not test or support merely to disclaim them. Put necessary scope boundaries and interpretation in Discussion, except when a negative or contrary result must be reported to represent the evidence faithfully.
11. Build the Introduction as a sequence of reader decisions: why the problem matters, which stage or context is decisive, why each central variable deserves attention, why those variables belong in one framework, what precise relationship remains unknown, and how the study resolves that gap. Introduce methods only after naming the scientific limitation they address.

## Figure and table numbering

Treat numbering as a first-citation invariant in the final manuscript, not as a property inherited from working files.

- Number main figures, main tables, supplementary figures, and supplementary tables in four independent sequences, each according to first in-text appearance.
- Cite a results-bearing supplementary table first in the Results section where its evidence is reported. Do not cite it prematurely in Methods merely to reserve a number.
- A supplementary table may first appear in Methods only when it is needed to define reproducible inputs or procedures, such as annotation markers, prespecified gene sets, primers, reagents, eligibility rules, or protocol parameters. Give it the next number at that actual Methods citation.
- When a table mixes method definitions with study-derived results, prefer separating the two if that reduces ambiguity without adding redundancy. If it remains mixed, treat it as results-bearing and first cite it in Results; describe the procedure in Methods without an early table citation.
- After moving or adding any citation, renumber the affected items and update in-text citations, filenames, table or figure titles, legends, supplementary indexes, and cross-references together.
- Before delivery, scan the complete final body in reading order. For each of the four sequences, verify that unique first appearances are monotonic and gap-free, and that every delivered item is cited and every citation resolves to a delivered item.

## Output contract

- Separate `Story diagnosis` from `Draft output` when both are requested.
- Name the central axis explicitly.
- For evidence or figure reviews, label the conclusion as `descriptive`, `supportive`, `mechanistically strong`, or `causally closed`.
- For journal-fit decisions, identify whether the main blocker is `logic`, `causality`, `validation`, or `finish`.
- For defensive-writing checks, report every issue in source order as `location / original phrase -> problem -> minimal revision`; do not rewrite unaffected text.
- For figure legends, describe only what is visibly encoded: lines or segments, symbols or shapes, colors, groups, axes, scale bars, statistical marks, and abbreviations. Do not interpret the result, state what the figure shows biologically, or repeat a Results-section conclusion.
- In figure legends, panel labels such as `a`, `b`, `c`, and `d` must match the labels printed in the figure exactly, including capitalization and numbering format. Never silently change `a` to `A`, `A` to `a`, or otherwise normalize the figure's labels.
- Remove project-management and diagnostic-manual wording from manuscript prose, including metaphorical uses of `threshold`, `freeze`, `feature panel`, `troubleshooting guide`, and bureaucratic `reported` constructions. Retain a term only when it is the technically correct description of a reproducible method, and then define it precisely.
- Prefer affirmative scope statements over defensive negation. Do not insert sentences such as `But this is not an equivalence test` or `However, this application does not support a universally invariant treatment-comparison magnitude` into Results unless the distinction is required to interpret the reported analysis correctly.
- Do not invent results, mechanisms, advantages, or tradeoff explanations. Narrow the claim when evidence is missing.

## Domain pairing

Use relevant analysis, statistics, wet-lab, structural-biology, or figure-production skills when the manuscript judgment depends on technical validation. Keep those domain workflows separate from this writing and manuscript-development skill.
