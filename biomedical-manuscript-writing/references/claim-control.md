# Claim Control

## Purpose

Use this file to keep claims proportional to evidence while preserving a strong manuscript voice.

## Evidence Ladder

### Descriptive

Evidence examples:

- expression correlation
- enrichment analysis
- observational single-cell clustering
- clinical co-occurrence without perturbation

Preferred verbs:

- associates with
- correlates with
- is linked to
- is enriched in
- implicates
- suggests

Avoid:

- drives
- controls
- determines
- mediates

### Supportive

Evidence examples:

- expression plus one orthogonal validation
- partial perturbation
- pathway consistency without full causal chain

Preferred verbs:

- promotes
- contributes to
- supports
- influences
- is required for, if the perturbation is direct and strong

### Mechanistically strong

Evidence examples:

- perturbation plus rescue or convergent orthogonal evidence
- lineage or origin evidence with functional follow-through
- upstream to downstream pathway control tied to phenotype

Preferred verbs:

- directs
- regulates
- controls
- orchestrates
- mediates
- establishes, if the paper truly closes the question

### Causally closed

Use when endogenous perturbation and rescue are strong and in-vivo and mechanistic readouts align.

Preferred verbs:

- drives
- mediates
- is required for

Do not assign this label merely because several associative datasets agree.

## Non-Negotiable Rules

### Single-cell rule

- single-cell or multi-omics data can prioritize a candidate
- they do not by themselves prove causality

### Model system rule

- mouse evidence supports relevance to mouse biology
- human translation needs either human data or explicitly cautious wording

### Biomarker rule

- if the marker tracks outcome, use `predicts` only when there is enough validation
- otherwise use `associates with outcome` or `correlates with response`

### Therapeutic rule

- if intervention was not directly tested, say `may offer a therapeutic avenue` rather than `provides a therapy`

## Common Overclaim Repairs

### Overclaim

- `Single-cell analysis revealed that X drives fibrosis.`

Repair:

- `Single-cell analysis identified X as a candidate regulator associated with fibrotic remodeling.`
- If functional data exist, then upgrade only the part that was truly tested.

### Overclaim

- `These findings establish a treatment strategy.`

Repair:

- `These findings identify a therapeutic axis worth further investigation.`

### Overclaim

- `Mouse data demonstrate direct human relevance.`

Repair:

- `These data support the translational relevance of the mouse model for the tested process.`

## Translational Language Ladder

Use the strongest accurate level supported by the evidence and the tested scope. Narrow the claim where necessary rather than adding defensive self-criticism.

- provides a resource
- supports model relevance
- suggests a therapeutic avenue
- identifies a candidate biomarker
- highlights a target worth further validation
- offers translational insight

Reserve stronger language for truly direct evidence.

## Sentence-Level Filters

Before finalizing a sentence, ask:

1. what exact evidence supports this verb?
2. is this a causal statement or an associative statement?
3. does the model system justify the scope of the conclusion?
4. is the last clause selling more than the sentence proved?

## Mentor-Style Preference

The corpus tends to prefer a confident but disciplined tone. The goal is not timid writing. The goal is to place the strongest accurate verb exactly where the evidence is strongest.

## Judgment labels

Useful concise verdicts include:

- `very strong`
- `strong but not closed`
- `clean but still supportive`
- `thin`
- `not main-text worthy yet`
- `good direction, wrong figure`

Avoid empty assessments such as `nice`, `interesting`, or `promising` without explaining the evidentiary consequence.
