# Figure Architecture

## General rules

- one main question per figure
- one visual centerpiece per figure
- one line of argument from left to right

Before accepting a figure, identify the one mechanistic sentence it enables. If one sentence cannot describe it, the figure is probably overloaded or contains two questions.

For each figure, identify:

1. the title sentence;
2. the visual centerpiece;
3. the supportive panels;
4. panels that belong in the supplement;
5. whether discovery and mechanism have been improperly mixed.

## Good figure sequence

1. discovery
2. mechanism
3. upstream licensing
4. downstream execution
5. perturbation or therapy

## Common failure modes

- discovery figures overloaded with mechanistic previews
- too many descriptive panels and no centerpiece
- weak public validation panels in the main text
- tiny unreadable heatmaps used to imply depth
- therapy panels that show tumor burden but not mechanism

## Preferred panel hierarchy

Main-text worthy:
- state-defining panel
- patient-level validation
- functional perturbation
- in vivo rescue or therapy effect

Supportive:
- public cohort support
- weak pathway enrichment
- illustrative spatial images without strong quantification
- gain-of-function assays in reductionist models

Supplement:
- extra UMAPs
- repetitive feature plots
- marginal external datasets
- crowded heatmaps that do not change the conclusion

## Analysis-specific cautions

- In single-cell studies, prioritize donor- or patient-level evidence over cell-count-only differences. Use public data to test conservation rather than force universality.
- Use bulk cohorts to support cell-state relevance, not to replace stronger single-cell biology. Treat optimal survival cutpoints as exploratory.
- Map every wet-lab assay to a precise mechanistic sentence. Describe an unvalidated blocking reagent as exploratory interference and a reductionist cell model as such.
- A therapy panel should connect intervention, phenotype, and the proposed mechanism; tumor burden alone does not close that chain.
