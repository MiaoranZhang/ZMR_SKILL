# Logic Framework

## Purpose

Use this file to build a clear, evidence-led scientific argument for a biomedical manuscript.

## Default Story Spine

### 1. Start with a gap that matters biologically

Preferred openings are not generic. They usually do one of these:

- define a disease or tissue problem with real consequences
- identify a missing mechanism behind a known phenotype
- point out a translational uncertainty between model and human systems
- emphasize heterogeneity that prevents clean interpretation

Good example shapes:

- tissue X has a distinctive phenotype, but the mechanism is unclear
- disease Y is clinically important, yet the decisive regulatory axis remains unresolved
- model Z is widely used, but its translational relevance is incompletely defined

### 2. Choose a high-information contrast

The corpus repeatedly uses a contrast that can force the biology to reveal itself:

- human versus mouse
- injured versus homeostatic tissue
- young versus aged
- sensitive versus resistant tumors
- low versus high malignancy
- wildtype versus perturbed regulator

If there is no sharp contrast, the story usually weakens.

### 3. Use an integrating platform to prioritize, not to conclude

Typical platforms:

- single-cell transcriptomics
- multi-omics integration
- clinical correlation
- lineage tracing
- organoid or functional perturbation

Rule:

- use the platform to narrow the field to one candidate axis
- do not let the platform become the final claim

### 4. Name the decisive entity early

The papers usually become sharper once one of these is named clearly:

- one regulator or signaling component
- one cell state or lineage
- one cellular source
- one origin claim
- one interaction axis

If the draft keeps introducing new candidates late, the logic is still unstable.

### 5. Build the mechanism bridge

The mechanistic layer often follows this order:

1. upstream trigger or context
2. regulator or niche interaction
3. signaling or transcriptional axis
4. cellular consequence
5. tissue or disease phenotype

This keeps the paper from sounding descriptive.

### 6. Prove function outside the discovery platform

Strong papers in this corpus rarely stop at association. They usually add one or more of:

- perturbation
- lineage evidence
- orthogonal assay
- clinical or disease relevance
- response prediction or therapeutic implication

### 7. End with a restrained implication

Preferred ending styles:

- reveals a therapeutic avenue
- provides a resource for translational studies
- supports model relevance with caveats
- identifies a biomarker or axis worth targeting

The ending should sound useful, not promotional.

## Figure Logic Template

For mechanistic papers, the default figure order is often:

1. phenotype, disease need, or atlas overview
2. candidate identification or cell-state prioritization
3. validation of the central entity
4. mechanistic dissection
5. functional consequence
6. translational or clinical extension

For atlas or comparative papers:

1. dataset integration and cell landscape
2. conserved structure or dominant cell classes
3. species-specific or condition-specific divergence
4. focused biological interpretation
5. translational boundary or utility statement

## Common Logic Failures

### Failure 1: The paper has data but no axis

Symptom:

- many findings appear equally important

Fix:

- choose one regulator, cell type, or interaction as the paper's central answer

### Failure 2: The paper has a mechanism word but no mechanism proof

Symptom:

- `mechanistically` is followed only by correlation

Fix:

- either add functional evidence or weaken the wording to `associated with` or `linked to`

### Failure 3: The translational statement outruns the model

Symptom:

- mouse or in vitro data are described as directly clinical

Fix:

- reframe as model-supported implication, not established human truth

### Failure 4: The manuscript sells method instead of biology

Symptom:

- the method appears in every sentence while the biological question remains vague

Fix:

- rewrite around the biological problem first and let the method appear as the route to the answer

## What This Mentor Logic Probably Rejects

- titles that are broad but noncommittal
- abstracts that mention too many unrelated molecules
- discussions that repeat results without synthesis
- strong causal verbs for evidence that is only associative
- conclusions that skip from one model to universal therapy claims
