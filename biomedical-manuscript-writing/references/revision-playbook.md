# Revision Playbook

## Purpose

Use this file to revise a draft into a clear, evidence-led biomedical manuscript.

Apply the manuscript principles in [../SKILL.md](../SKILL.md) throughout. For defensive-writing checks and minimal edits, follow that entrypoint's focused output contract; do not expand the task into the full five-pass revision below.

## Five-Pass Revision Sequence

### Pass 1: Story diagnosis

Ask:

- what is the single sentence answer of this paper?
- what is the central axis?
- what contrast makes the result legible?
- where does mechanism begin and where does association end?

If these are unclear, do not start polishing sentences yet.

### Pass 2: Title and abstract compression

Ask:

- does the title name the real core of the paper?
- does the abstract follow important problem, existing gap, paper's approach, and strongest supported result, with mechanism and implication where supported?
- is there exactly one main axis in the abstract?

### Pass 3: Section alignment

Ask:

- does each result subsection answer one question?
- do the introduction and discussion support the same story rather than different stories?
- are figure order and result order aligned?

### Pass 4: Claim control

Ask:

- is each causal verb earned?
- is translational language proportional to the model?
- are there statements that should be weakened from `drives` to `promotes`, or from `establishes` to `suggests`?

### Pass 5: Sentence tightening

Prefer:

- shorter subject-verb-object structure
- one mechanism per sentence
- fewer stacked subordinate clauses
- less adjective inflation

## Common Revision Moves

### If the draft feels broad but weak

- narrow the story to one regulator or one cell-state transition
- demote secondary findings to support

### If the draft feels descriptive

- insert the question each result answers
- make the mechanism bridge explicit

### If the discussion feels repetitive

- replace result restatement with synthesis and field placement

### If the title feels generic

- swap broad nouns for the actual decisive axis

## Mentor-Style Editing Preferences

- strong nouns and verbs beat decorative adjectives
- one memorable mechanistic sentence is better than three vague significance sentences
- the final sentence should open a future direction without pretending the paper solved everything

## Reviewer Response Style

When drafting responses to reviewers:

- answer the criticism directly in the first sentence
- state what was changed and where
- defend with evidence, not emotion
- concede limitations cleanly when the reviewer is correct
- avoid sounding wounded or argumentative

Template:

1. thank the reviewer briefly
2. state the concern accurately
3. answer with data or revision
4. state the manuscript location changed

## Fast Prompt Patterns

Use these prompt goals when helping the user:

- `diagnose the story before rewriting`
- `rewrite the abstract around one central axis`
- `give me three title options with different claim strength`
- `reorder the results around one central mechanistic question`
- `weaken overclaims without losing impact`
- `draft a reviewer response in direct evidence-based tone`
