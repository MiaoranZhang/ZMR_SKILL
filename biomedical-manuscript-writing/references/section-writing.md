# Section Writing

## Purpose

Use this file when outlining or drafting biomedical manuscript sections.

## Introduction Logic

For full Introduction design, including dual-line convergence and multimodal studies, read [introduction-writing.md](introduction-writing.md).

### Default three-paragraph introduction for a mechanistic paper

Paragraph 1:

- define the biological or clinical importance of the system
- narrow to the specific unresolved problem

Paragraph 2:

- summarize what is already known about the relevant cells, regulators, or pathways
- identify the exact gap, contradiction, or translational limit

Paragraph 3:

- state the study strategy and the main question
- avoid giving away every result
- position the paper around the central axis

### Introduction habits from the corpus

- move from tissue or disease context to cell-state or regulatory uncertainty
- motivate the model system rather than assuming it is self-evident
- when comparing species, explicitly state why model-to-human translation is uncertain
- make each paragraph perform one argumentative function; do not organize background by literature category alone
- introduce a method as the answer to a named scientific limitation, not as an additional technology
- end with the central question and study logic rather than a chronological `First... Then... Next... Finally...` workflow report

## Results Logic

### Preferred structure

Each subsection should answer one question.

Template:

1. heading states the biological answer or the tested relation
2. first sentence reminds the reader why this step matters
3. middle sentences present the evidence in a logical order
4. last sentence interprets what this subsection establishes

### Common results progression

- identify the phenotype or system-level pattern
- isolate the key cell type, regulator, or niche interaction
- verify the axis with orthogonal evidence
- test function or causality
- extend to disease relevance, repair, or response prediction

### Results inclusion discipline

- Report what was tested, observed, and supported; do not turn Results into a catalog of untested or unsupported interpretations.
- Omit defensive sentences that merely announce what an analysis was not designed to establish.
- Put necessary generalizability, equivalence, invariance, and transportability boundaries in Discussion unless they are required to interpret the analysis itself.
- Report negative, null, or contrary results in Results when they materially constrain the central conclusion, correspond to prespecified endpoints, or are required by reporting standards. Keep their broader interpretation in Discussion.
- Use scientific nouns and verbs rather than project-management terms, internal workflow labels, or troubleshooting language.

### Result heading style

Preferred headings usually sound interpretive rather than merely procedural.

Better:

- `HOXC9 promotes OSCC invasion through the ITGA6 PI3K Akt MMP13 axis`
- `Macrophage-derived IL-1 activates fibroblast progenitors during oral wound repair`

Worse:

- `RNA-seq analysis`
- `Validation experiment`

## Discussion Logic

### Default discussion structure

Paragraph 1:

- answer the main question directly in one or two sentences
- state the central advance

Paragraph 2:

- integrate the finding into the existing field
- explain how it extends, refines, or challenges prior understanding

Paragraph 3:

- discuss the mechanism or biological logic at a higher level
- connect cell state, niche, and phenotype if relevant

Paragraph 4:

- state translational or conceptual implications
- mention limitations without collapsing the paper's value
- express necessary boundaries as the tested scope and the next unresolved question, rather than stacking defensive negations

### Discussion habits from the corpus

- emphasize why the identified axis matters for regeneration, malignancy, fibrosis, or therapy response
- keep the discussion tied to the same central axis used in the title and abstract
- use comparative papers to define where translation is reliable and where caution is needed

## Review Article Structure

### Recommended logic

1. define the field and its importance
2. explain why the topic is complex, plastic, or contradictory
3. organize sections by subtype, mechanism, or biological role
4. compare pro and anti effects when the field is bidirectional
5. end with challenges, translational potential, and future directions

### Review-writing habits from the corpus

- use contrast and duality to structure the field
- prefer synthesis over cataloging
- keep therapeutic implications visible but not detached from mechanism

## Figure Storytelling

### Figure order for a mechanistic manuscript

- Figure 1: context, phenotype, or atlas overview
- Figure 2: identify the key regulator or cell population
- Figure 3: validate the association in orthogonal assays
- Figure 4: define the mechanism
- Figure 5: show functional consequence
- Figure 6: extend to translational or clinical relevance

### Figure legend style

- Treat the legend as a decoding key for the figure, not as a miniature Results section.
- Describe only what is displayed: the meaning of lines or line segments, symbols, shapes, colors, groups, horizontal and vertical axes, scale bars, statistical symbols, and abbreviations.
- Define all abbreviations and non-obvious visual encodings needed to read the panel.
- Reproduce every panel label exactly as it appears in the figure. Labels such as `a`, `b`, `c`, and `d` must preserve capitalization and numbering format; do not convert them to `A`, `B`, `C`, and `D`, or vice versa.
- Before delivery, compare the legend against the figure panel by panel and verify that every cited label exists, appears in the correct order, and matches character for character.
- Do not explain the biological meaning of a pattern, state what result the figure reflects, infer a mechanism, or add a conclusion.
- Do not repeat Results-section interpretation in the legend.

## Paragraph-Level Writing Rules

- keep each paragraph focused on one claim
- put the topic sentence early
- avoid ending paragraphs on raw data description; end on interpretation
- keep transition sentences functional rather than decorative
